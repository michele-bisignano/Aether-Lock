## 1. Physical System Modeling

The system consists of a ferromagnetic object (the "Angel") suspended by an electromagnet. The objective is to control the vertical position of the object by modulating the current flowing through the coil.

### 1.1 Coordinates and Forces
We define the vertical axis $x$ pointing **downwards**, with the origin $x=0$ located at the bottom face of the electromagnet.

*   $x(t)$: Vertical position of the object [m]
*   $i(t)$: Current in the electromagnet coil [A]

The forces acting on the object are:
1.  **Gravity ($F_g$):** Acts downwards (positive $x$ direction).
    $$ F_g = m \cdot g $$
2.  **Magnetic Force ($F_m$):** Acts upwards (negative $x$ direction). Since the object is a permanent magnet, the force is proportional to the current (not squared) and inversely proportional to the distance raised to a power $n$ (typically 3 or 4 for dipole interactions).
    $$ F_m(x, i) = K_{mag} \frac{i(t)}{x(t)^n} $$

### 1.2 Non-Linear Differential Equation
Applying Newton's Second Law ($F = ma$):

$$ m \ddot{x}(t) = m g - K_{mag} \frac{i(t)}{x(t)^n} $$

This is a **Non-Linear Differential Equation**.

### 1.4 Advanced Modeling (Ground Truth)

To validate the simplified model ($F \propto i/x^n$), we derive the exact magnetic force using the Biot-Savart law for a finite solenoid acting on a magnetic dipole.

The force acting on the permanent magnet (dipole) is given by:
$$ \mathbf{F} = \nabla (\mathbf{m} \cdot \mathbf{B}) $$

$$ F_x = m_{mag} \cdot \frac{dB_x}{dx} $$

Where:
*   $m_{mag}$: Magnetic Dipole Moment of the Angel [$A \cdot m^2$].
*   $\frac{dB_x}{dx}$: Gradient of the magnetic field generated by the coil along the vertical axis.

For a solenoid of Length $L$, Radius $R$, and Total Turns $N$, the field gradient along the central axis at distance $x$ from the face is:

$$ \frac{dB_x}{dx} = - \frac{d}{dx} \left\{\frac{\mu_0 n i}{2} \left[ \frac{L/2 + x}{\sqrt{R^2 + (L/2+x)^2}} - \frac{x - L/2}{\sqrt{R^2 + (x - L/2)^2}} \right] \right\} $$

$$ \frac{dB_x}{dx} = -4n \mu_0 IR^2 \left[ \frac{1}{\sqrt{(4R^2 + 4x^2 + 4LX + L^2)^3}} - \frac{1}{\sqrt{(4R^2 + 4x^2 - 4LX + L^2)^3}} \right]$$

*Note: The user-derived formula in the notes corresponds to the derivative of the algebraic form of this expression.*

This complex formula will be used in MATLAB to perform **Curve Fitting** and identify the optimal parameters ($K_{mag}$ and exponent $n$) for the simplified design model used in the firmware.
